01 · About me

I start with reality,then design.

My training in environmental design taught me to notice people, space and behaviour. Product work taught me how to translate those observations into requirements, flows, prototypes and decisions that can be tested. I care less about ideas that merely look right, and more about whether they answer a real situation, make sense to a team and can move forward.

CASE 01

AI Product · Topic Owner

AI Quick Edit for TV

Across TCL’s TV, mobile and connected-home ecosystem, I explored opportunities in generative AI and imaging, while owning the product direction for an AI-assisted video editing concept on TV.

My role

I shaped the product judgement through users, contexts, device strengths and technical maturity; aligned research, algorithm, client, embedded, design and business teams; and used scenario walkthroughs and sample comparisons to refine the experience.

Takeaway

An AI product is not a list of capabilities. It is a sequence of trade-offs between technical possibility, user value and delivery reality.

CASE 02

AI Education · Product Intern

A Multi-role Education Product Loop

Across three AI education products, I worked with the needs of teachers, students, operations and customer support, translating scattered feedback into clear business rules, flows and acceptance criteria.

My role

I maintained the demand pool and release scope, wrote PRDs, mapped edge cases and built prototypes. Usage data and frontline feedback helped me locate friction in the core journey and move requests from “someone asked” to “the team can validate it.”

Takeaway

A strong requirement is not longer. It gives different roles one shared understanding of the problem.

04 · Notes

Notes stillin progress.

These are not finished conclusions. They are questions that keep returning in my projects — and keep changing how I work.

FIELD NOTE 01

Before “what can AI do?”, ask “why is it needed?”

Capability matters. But the person, moment and context determine whether a capability can become a product.

FIELD NOTE 02

Spatial design was my first product language.

Movement, scale, behaviour and feeling are all parts of an experience. They taught me to begin with real contexts, not abstract features.

FIELD NOTE 03

Making complexity clear is a form of creation.

When research, technology, business and experience pull in different directions, a precise problem is often more valuable than a fast answer.
